fleetd runs Claude Code on your machines: you type what you want, and it plans the work, runs the agents, opens pull requests it has checked, and asks you only about real decisions.

An agent saying “done” doesn’t merge anything. Every change goes through fleetd’s merge train: reviewed, rebased onto main, merged only on green CI.

curl -fsSL https://fleetd.sh | sh

Installs and sets up fleetd: about a minute later the hub runs, the app is paired, and you type your first order. You need macOS or Linux x64, the claude CLI logged in (your own claude setup, proxy included), git and gh. MIT.

What the lead does without asking you.

Taken from the same run: the lead’s commands, the hub’s events and the lead’s own replies.

  1. The lead decides everything that isn’t a product, money, credential or irreversible call.
  2. The lead files the rest with fleetd ask, each with a class, a recommendation and what it blocks, and carries on without waiting.
  3. The lead hands workstreams and tasks to Claude Code agents on your machines.
  4. The lead starts every wake from the hub’s digest: answers, open workstreams, tasks, open asks, decisions.
  5. The lead doesn’t re-ask a key that’s already decided.
  6. The lead keeps your answer when it overrides its own recommendation.
  7. The lead gets an incident, I1 misfiled_ask, when you drop an ask it could have decided itself.

Setup is a hub and your machines.

After the install, fleetd init walks you through the hub, its claude and gh logins and the desktop app’s pairing. fleetd machine add turns a machine you can reach over SSH into one that runs agents. Both outputs are real; the tailnet name and address are placeholders.
