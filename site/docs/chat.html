Chat with the lead

You’ll give a project work by chatting with its lead, the way you’d talk to an engineer.

Each project has one lead and one ongoing chat. Click the project in the sidebar to open it.

Send a message

Section titled “Send a message”

Type in the box at the bottom of the chat and press Enter. Shift+Enter adds a new line.

You can How
Describe work In your own words. The lead turns it into tasks itself.
Attach files Paste, drop, or attach images, text or PDF. The lead gets them with your message.
Paste a screenshot Paste it into the box.

The lead keeps its own plan and bookkeeping. You never file tasks or orders yourself.

Reply to the lead

Section titled “Reply to the lead”

The lead asks only what it really needs: product calls, money, and irreversible steps. It asks in the chat, as numbered items with a recommendation each:

Reply in plain words, or tersely:

Reply Means
1 go, 2 rec Yes to item 1, take the recommendation on item 2.
do it Go ahead with what it proposed.
nvm Drop it.
2: keep it for two releases Answer an item in your own words.

The lead logs each answer as a decision, so it never asks the same thing twice. A project shows a dot in the sidebar while the lead’s last message waits for your reply, or a secret or approval item is open.

Steer while it works

Section titled “Steer while it works”

A message reaches the lead at once. How depends on what the lead is doing:

The lead is Your message
Idle Starts its next turn.
Working Is a steer: it reads it at its next step, after the tool it is running. The box says so while you type.
Starting, or its machine is away Is held, and goes in as soon as it can. The chat says why it waits.

A message is never lost: if it can’t be delivered, it stays held, the chat says why, and fleetd keeps trying.

A small receipt under your message shows when it was taken in. See Steering.

Follow the work

Section titled “Follow the work”

Everything shows up in the chat, quietly:

In the chat Shows
The agents strip, under the lead’s message One line: how many agents are working, and on which PRs. Expand it to see each agent’s role, what it’s doing and for how long. Click an agent waiting on you to jump to what it needs.
One-line events A pull request opened, merged, or sent back.

For a project’s progress at a glance, see Status. For where the time went, see Where time went.

Earlier work

Section titled “Earlier work”

Work from before the chat existed shows at the top of each project’s chat, under “From before this chat”: what you asked for, the lead’s replies, and how each piece of work ended, such as “Done: 2 PRs merged.” or “Stopped by you; kept for later.” Nothing was moved or deleted. Scroll up to read it.

Stop everything

Section titled “Stop everything”

To stop all of a project’s work at once, say “stop” in the chat, or run:

From an MCP client, call project_stop. Every running and queued agent is stopped, and the lead says so in the chat. Merged pull requests stay, and so do the lead and the chat: write again to start new work.

Secrets and risky commands

Section titled “Secrets and risky commands”

Two things never go through plain chat text:

Item What you see What you do
A secret the lead needs A masked field naming the machine and where the value goes Paste or type the value and click “Save secret”. It never enters the chat.
A risky command The exact command, why, and where it runs Approve or Deny.

See Secrets & approvals.

Done, checked

Section titled “Done, checked”

The lead can’t just say “done”. When it claims the work is finished, the hub runs the work’s done-when checks itself, and only all of them passing makes it done. A check that needs you is one line in the chat, such as “looks good on your end?”.

Work never closes over something that hasn’t landed: a writer still at work or failed (a review’s fix, say), or a pull request not merged yet. The lead says so in the chat and claims again once it lands. If some of it is truly not needed, the lead closes over it on purpose and tells you why; the record keeps what was dropped and why.

When work is done, its pull requests still waiting are closed on GitHub with a comment saying why, and its leftover agents stop, except writers whose work hasn’t landed, which keep going.
