The desktop app

You’ll pair the desktop app with your hub and use it to chat with each project’s lead.

The app is installed with fleetd on any machine with a display. To add it on Linux without a display, see Install. It updates itself (updating); fleetd update updates it too. Shortcuts below use ⌘; on Linux, use Ctrl.

Pair it with the hub

Section titled “Pair it with the hub”

The first time it opens, the app says what fleetd is and shows three steps that tick themselves as you go: connect to your hub, add a project, say hi to its lead.

With no hub on this computer yet, click “Set up fleetd on this Mac”: it opens fleetd init in Terminal (on Linux, your terminal). When setup finishes, come back to the app and it pairs by itself. If no terminal opens, the app shows the command to copy. On the hub machine, the app pairs by itself when it opens. On another computer:

  1. On the hub, print a pairing code:

  2. In the app, under “Or connect to a hub on another computer”, enter the hub address and the code. Or open the fleetd://pair link.

The code works once, for 15 minutes. Capitals and the dash are optional: abcd2345 works as ABCD-2345. To connect with a key instead, click “Use a key instead”.

The app gets admin on every machine, so it changes the hub’s settings, such as max agents. For a device that should only watch or only drive agents, print its code with fleetd pair --role read or --role operate. If Settings says “This key lacks the admin role”, the app was paired with less: run fleetd pair on the hub machine and pair again with the new code.

To change hubs, open Settings (⌘,) → General → Hub.

The window

Section titled “The window”
Part Shows
Sidebar Your projects, each saying why it wants you: “reply”, “approve”, “secret” or “blocked”. Otherwise “CI red” while a check fails on main (which blocks nothing), or how many agents are working. A project in bold has a new message from its lead. Below them: Status, Time, Machines and Settings.
The top row The page’s title and its controls, then Commands. In a chat: the project’s name, its state word and its Max agents control, and the panel’s toggle.
The chat The selected project’s conversation with its lead.
The panel Beside a chat, on the right: a tab each for the project’s agents, its pull requests, its merge train, its checklist, its activity and its decisions. Open it with the toggle at the top right or ⌥⌘B.

In a window narrower than about 1000 px the sidebar and the panel hide themselves; ⌘B and ⌥⌘B show them again. The app remembers whether you left the panel open, the tab you had and its width. On macOS the Dock icon shows how many projects need you.

The panel

Section titled “The panel”

Drag the panel’s left edge to make it wider or narrower; double-click the edge to put it back. From the keyboard, Tab to the edge, then ← widens it and → narrows it (with Shift, in bigger steps), Home and End go to its narrowest and widest, and Enter resets it. Reading a pull request’s changes widens the panel so the diff has room; that wide width is set the same way and kept apart from the other. The panel never grows so wide that the chat loses its room.

When the tabs don’t fit, they show as icons with their counts (point at one for its name), and if even those don’t fit, the rest go under More (···). The tab you’re on always stays in view. Each tab is a link too: #/project/<machine>/<project>/checklist opens the checklist, as the palette’s Checklist does, and /train, /activity and /decisions open those tabs the same way.

Click a project to open its chat. ⌥⌘A jumps to the next project that wants you. To add a project, click “Add” next to Projects, then paste a GitHub URL or owner/repo. You can also drop a folder or a GitHub link anywhere on the window, a chat included. Other files dropped on a chat attach to your message. See Add a project.

The chat

Section titled “The chat”
Part Does
Messages The conversation, with Markdown, attachments and pasted images. Older history loads as you scroll up, including work from before the chat existed. A chat opens where you stopped reading, under a “New” line. Scrolled up, a button takes you back to the newest message and counts what arrived meanwhile.
The box at the bottom Your message (“Message the lead”), ready to type as soon as a chat opens. Enter sends; Shift+Enter adds a line; ↑ in an empty box brings back your last message; the clip or ⌘⇧A attaches a file. While the lead works, a message steers it, and Stop beside Send ends its turn now. Unsent words stay, even after you quit.
The line above the box While the lead waits on you, what for (“2 questions, asked at 09:02”, “1 command to approve”). Show scrolls to it.
An empty chat Asks what the lead should do, with a few starters that fill the box for you to edit.
The agents strip Under the lead’s message, after any approval or secret it asks for: how many agents are working, and on which PRs. Click it to open Agents; click a PR number to open the PR.
One-line events Pull requests opened, merged or sent back. A PR’s number or GitHub link here or in a message opens the PR in the panel.
Secret and approval items A masked field with “Save secret”, or a command (or a tool’s permission request) with Approve and Deny. See Secrets & approvals.
The top row The project, its state word (as in Status), and its Max agents control (“agents 6 / 12”).

When the hub is out of reach

Section titled “When the hub is out of reach”

Your projects and recent chats stay on screen, marked “Offline, as of 09:02”. You can keep writing: each message shows “queued” and sends by itself when the app reconnects, once, even if you quit in between. Edit or Dismiss takes a queued message back. The banner says why the hub doesn’t answer and when the app tries again; Retry now tries at once. Approvals, secrets and Stop wait until the hub is back.

Once you’re connected again, a chat says “Last known” until its newest messages load; if they can’t, it says so, with Retry. “Disconnect and forget key” in Settings clears what the app kept for that hub, queued messages included.

How to work with the lead: Chat with the lead.

Agents

Section titled “Agents”

The Agents tab of the panel beside a chat (⌥⌘B) lists the project’s agents at work, those waiting on you first: each one’s role, its task, what it’s doing and for how long. Click one to watch its work as it happens: what it was told, the commands it ran, the files it edited and what it’s writing. Watching doesn’t interrupt it. To change what it does, tell the lead in the chat. Its PR number opens the PR, and What it was told shows the exact first message and prompts it started with; All agents or Esc goes back to the list.

All projects, above the list, lists every project’s agents instead, each project under its name, those with an agent waiting on you first. Click an agent of another project to open its work beside that project’s chat. This project goes back. The panel remembers which you chose.

Train

Section titled “Train”

The Train tab shows the project’s merge train: the pull request merging now and its stage (rebasing onto the base, running CI, merging) with the checks it waits on, then the pull requests in line, in the order they’ll merge. Each one says whether it’s waiting for review, in review, ready, or blocked, and what blocks it: a hold label, a conflict with the base, or failed checks that sent it back to the agent that wrote it. Pull requests that go through together as a batch show as one group, while they merge and after. Below them are the latest merges and refusals, with the fix a refusal asks for. Click any of them to open the pull request. The tab follows the train as it moves, shows when each thing happened and never guesses how long something will take. Its count is the pull requests merging or in line. It only reads: the train itself decides what merges.

Checklist

Section titled “Checklist”

The Checklist tab shows the project’s to-dos as its lead keeps them: how many are done, then what’s being done now, what’s still to do and, folded away, what’s done. An item’s pull request or agent opens in the panel. The lead updates the list as the work moves, and the tab follows along as it does. The tab’s count is the items still open.

You can’t edit the list yourself: to change it, tell the lead in the chat. A project’s lead starts a checklist when it plans work; until then the tab says there is none yet.

Activity

Section titled “Activity”

The Activity tab shows what happened in the project while you were away, newest first: agents starting and finishing, reviews, merges, what was asked of you and what you answered. A line marks what came in since you last looked, and a run of the same thing (a pull request reviewed again and again) folds into one row. A line that names an agent or a pull request opens it. The tab’s count is what came in since you last looked.

Decisions

Section titled “Decisions”

The Decisions tab lists what the lead and you have decided in the project, one row per question with the answer in force first. What it replaced, or that you revoked it, folds under it. Search, or narrow the list to who decided and where it applies; open a row for its why and history, and change or revoke the decision there. The tab’s count is the decisions in force.

Status

Section titled “Status”

Status shows one row per project: a state word (waiting on you, blocked, working or idle), its work in groups (waiting on you, in review, working, landing, idle, done), the agents running, and when the last pull request merged. Under it, each item opens its agent or pull request in the panel. Click a row to open that project’s chat, or PRs for its pull requests. See Status.

Where time went

Section titled “Where time went”

Open Time from the sidebar, the agents strip in a chat, or the command palette. It has three tabs:

Tab Shows
Timeline One piece of work: a lane per agent, PR and CI check, and the waits on you, with the critical path marked. Each agent’s lane shows its model and output tokens. Hover a bar for the command, its duration and exit code; a transcript’s “Open the chat” goes to the project’s chat.
Breakdown Agent time by group, with the slowest commands and CI checks.
Insights Findings, each with the spans behind it.

Times are in your own time zone. How to read it: Where time went.

Machines

Section titled “Machines”

Open Machines from the sidebar or the command palette. The title shows how many agents run across the fleet (“agents 2 / 12”), beside Add project.

Each machine is one row: its name, its state (online, degraded, or offline and when it was last seen), how many agents run there and how many projects it has. On the right, anything that wants a look: a recent sleep, Claude usage near its limit, a setup check that failed. A degraded machine says why under its name. Click a row to open it: load, memory, Claude usage, its fleetd version and tags, its projects (each with Open, Settings and Remove) and Roles and screens, which goes to Settings › Machines.

Under the machines, the setup checks the hub ran: the ones that failed or warned, each with its fix (a command to copy, or Fix), then the rest a click away. Re-run runs them again.

Settings

Section titled “Settings”

Open Settings with ⌘,. It takes the sidebar’s place; Back returns to where you were. Type in “Search settings” to find an option or a prompt by name, and press Enter to open the first match.

Section Has
General Appearance (light, dark, match system) and the hub connection.
Notifications Which alerts show (the lead asks you something, a command or secret waits, the lead finishes what you asked, a machine problem, anything else), whether they play a sound, the Dock badge, and a test. There is never an alert for the chat in front of you, and in quiet hours only urgent ones show. Clicking one opens what it’s about.
Agents Max agents for each project, each machine and the whole fleet. A machine says where its number comes from (“Auto: memory 60 GB, about 2 GB an agent”, or set by you), and “Use auto” goes back. Each slider applies when you let go, with Undo. A project says whether its max is its own or follows the default, and “Use the default” goes back. Below them, the two task profiles and each project’s Medium allowed switch.
Projects One project’s name, its worktrees, its agents’ environment variables (plain and secret), its own MCP servers, its setup commands, the merge train’s checks, and deleting it. See Project settings.
MCP servers The MCP servers every project’s agents get, each with Test connection, and whether agents also get your own. See MCP servers.
Instructions Your own text, added to every agent’s first message.
Prompts The prompts fleetd sends agents, listed under Prompts in the sidebar, to read and edit, and any of your edits that no longer apply. See What agents are told.

Shortcuts

Section titled “Shortcuts”

⌘/ shows every shortcut in the app, and ⌘K finds any command by name. ⌘ is Ctrl on Linux.

The command palette (⌘K) opens on the project you’re in. Type a setting’s own name (“max agents”, “dark”) to find it. What you open most rises to the top. Press ⌘K again to see the other actions for the selected item, such as a project’s instructions.
