Navi

A Navi is a personal assistant. It chats with you, remembers what matters, keeps your to-dos, reads the web, uses screens, and works in a folder of its own. Make as many as you like: one for home, one for taxes, one for a side project. Each is separate, with its own chat, memory and folder.

Under the hood a Navi is a project with no repo, whose lead runs as an assistant. So it gets what every project has, unchanged: chat, memory and its nightly refresh, automations, screens, the checklist and approvals.

In the app

Section titled “In the app”

Your Navis are pinned at the top of the sidebar, above Projects. With none yet, the sidebar shows Meet Navi. It opens a page that says what Navi does, with a name box and one button, Create your Navi. Leave the name empty and it’s called Navi. Its chat opens once it’s ready.

  • New beside the Navi heading makes another one.
  • A Navi’s right-click menu has Rename…, Open settings, Copy folder path and Archive.
  • Archive takes effect at once, with Undo in the notice. Archived Navis are listed on the Navi page (New opens it), each with Bring back.
  • ⌘1–9 (Ctrl on Linux and Windows) number the Navis first, then the projects.

A Navi’s chat is the project chat, framed for an assistant. The title is its name, and the box says Message Ada. The gear beside its name opens its settings, where its memory and automations are kept. A send or any other approval shows in the chat with Approve and Deny. The sidebar marks the Navi approve until you answer.

The panel beside the chat has a Navi’s own sections:

Section Shows
Asked The work this Navi asked your other projects for, newest first. Each item shows the project (click to open its chat), when it was asked, the Navi’s words, and each order opened for it (In progress with its latest headline, Done or Stopped).
Screens The browsers and devices it uses, once your hub serves screens.
Plan Its checklist.
History What happened, and what you and the Navi decided.

Asked shows on a hub that serves navi_asks. On any other hub, a Navi still tells you in its chat when work it asked for lands.

Make one from the terminal

Section titled “Make one from the terminal”

The Navi’s lead starts with your first message. Its folder is navi/<name> under the machine’s fleetd home, made on the spot. A second Navi with the same name gets ada-2. --machine <id> picks the machine; by default it’s the least busy worker.

Command Does
fleetd navi create <name> Make a Navi.
fleetd navi list [--archived] The Navis, oldest first; --archived includes archived ones.
fleetd navi rename <machine>/<navi> <name> Rename it. Its folder keeps its first name.
fleetd navi archive <machine>/<navi> Stop its lead and set it aside. Its chat, memory and folder stay.
fleetd navi unarchive <machine>/<navi> Bring it back. Its lead starts with your next message.

<navi> is its id or its name. A message to an archived Navi waits until you bring it back.

What it does on its own, and what it asks first

Section titled “What it does on its own, and what it asks first”

A Navi searches the web freely, and reads your connected mail, calendar and files and the factory’s status. It also writes drafts and notes in its folder, remembers and forgets, keeps its checklist, works screens and logs in with 1Password.

  • Files: it reads and writes only inside its own folder. A read or an edit anywhere else is refused, whoever approves it, so your keys, its MCP servers’ tokens and other projects stay out of its reach. It can’t change its own Claude Code settings, MCP servers or fleetd policy in its folder either (.claude, .mcp.json, .fleetd), and a policy file’s allow rules don’t apply to a Navi at all.
  • Sites: the first time it fetches a page or opens a site in its browser, you’re asked in its chat, with the site named. Approve once and it fetches and opens that site unasked from then on, with a plain URL: one with ? or &, quotes or other shell characters in a browser command asks each time. www. is the same site, a subdomain is another, and localhost is a site per port (localhost:3000). Only http and https name a site: file:, data:, about: and other schemes ask every time. The MCP tools navi_sites and navi_site_revoke list a Navi’s sites and take one back; a revoked site asks again.

Anything hard to undo, or that someone else sees, waits for you every time: sending an email or message, accepting an invite, spending, posting publicly, deleting, signing or accepting terms, changing a password. There is no “always allow”.

This is enforced outside the model. A Navi’s lead runs in Claude Code’s manual permission mode with a short allow-list (Agent tools). Its file tools and fetches always raise a permission prompt, whatever your own Claude Code settings allow. The node answers the file tools. Every other prompt goes to the hub, which allows a site you allowed before. Anything else becomes an approval in the Navi’s chat, with the tool and exactly what it would get: the recipients, the full text. Approve runs the tool with exactly that input. Deny tells the Navi you said no, with your reason if you give one. A prompt that goes away on its own (the run ended) closes its approval. Moving money, trading and creating accounts through an integration are blocked even if approved.

What screens can still do. The site gate covers opening a site; it doesn’t follow a page after that. Plain browser commands inside an open page run unasked: clicks, and fills and typing whose text is plain words or one single-quoted string (fill @e2 'Ana Ruiz'). So screens aren’t held to the approval rule for sends, and a click or a form that leads to another site isn’t asked. Its Android, iOS and desktop screens (agent-device) don’t ask per site at all. Nor is the final Send or Pay button on a site you allowed: that final click is left to you by instruction only. The Navi is told to hand it to you with a takeover, but that’s its prompt, not fleetd’s rules. Only allow sites where that’s fine, such as your bank’s when you mean to watch.

Anything else in a browser command asks: other quoting (double quotes, a quote joined to more text), a selector with brackets, *, ~ or braces, shell syntax, a script in the page (wait --fn, eval), or a flag that changes which browser runs or how (--session, --cdp, --headers).

Memory

Section titled “Memory”

A Navi keeps your private life (family, health, money, accounts) in its own memory, never in user.md. Every project’s lead reads user.md, so it holds only how you like to work. The nightly refresh tidies a Navi’s memory as it does any project’s.

For the app and other clients

Section titled “For the app and other clients”

Navis are MCP tools on the hub: navi_create {name, machineId?}, navi_list {archived?, machineId?}, navi_rename {machineId, projectId, name}, navi_archive {machineId, projectId, archived?}, navi_sites {machineId, projectId} and navi_site_revoke {machineId, projectId, site}. Each returns the Navi as a project summary with kind: "assistant", createdBy (the id and name of the key that made it) and, once archived, archivedAt. Every other project has no kind. Talk to a Navi with chat_send, as to any project. navi_asks {navi} lists what a Navi asked other projects for, with the orders opened for each, on a hub whose ready frame names the navi_asks feature.
