Navi

A Navi is a personal assistant. It chats with you, remembers what matters, keeps your to-dos, reads the web, uses screens, and works in a folder of its own. Make as many as you like: one for home, one for taxes, one for a side project. Each is separate, with its own chat, memory and folder.

Under the hood a Navi is a project with no repo, whose lead runs as an assistant. So it gets what every project has, unchanged: chat, memory and its nightly refresh, automations, screens, the checklist and approvals. You can also talk to it from Telegram.

Yours alone

Section titled “Yours alone”

Each Navi belongs to the person who made it. Only you see your Navis, in the sidebar, on the Navi page and under Hubs › Hub links. Only you use them: their chat and approvals, integrations and other tools, hub links, allowed sites, sign-ins, settings, archive and delete. The hub refuses everyone else, admins included. An admin can only delete the Navi of someone removed from the hub. On a hub from before people, Navis made earlier belong to the person whose device made them, else the hub’s owner.

In the app

Section titled “In the app”

Your Navis are pinned at the top of the sidebar, above Projects. With none yet, the sidebar shows Meet Navi. It opens a page that says what Navi does, with a name box and one button, Create your Navi. Leave the name empty and it’s called Navi. Its chat opens once it’s ready.

  • New beside the Navi heading makes another one.
  • A Navi’s right-click menu has Rename…, Open settings, Copy folder path and Archive.
  • Archive takes effect at once, with Undo in the notice. Archived Navis are listed on the Navi page (New opens it), each with Bring back.
  • ⌘1–9 (Ctrl on Linux and Windows) number the Navis first, then the projects.

A Navi’s chat is the project chat, framed for an assistant. The title is its name, and the box says Message Ada. The gear beside its name opens its settings, where its memory and automations are kept. A send or any other approval shows in the chat with Approve and Deny. The sidebar marks the Navi approve until you answer.

The panel beside the chat has a Navi’s own sections:

Section Shows
Asked The work this Navi asked your other projects for, newest first. Each item shows the project (click to open its chat), when it was asked, the Navi’s words, and each order opened for it (In progress with its latest headline, Done or Stopped).
Screens The browsers and devices it uses, once your hub serves screens.
Plan Its checklist.
History What happened, and what you and the Navi decided.

Asked shows on a hub that serves navi_asks. On any other hub, a Navi still tells you in its chat when work it asked for lands.

Make one from the terminal

Section titled “Make one from the terminal”

The Navi’s lead starts with your first message. Its folder is navi/<name> under the machine’s fleetd home, made on the spot. A second Navi with the same name gets ada-2. --machine <id> picks the machine; by default it’s the least busy worker.

Command Does
fleetd navi create <name> Make a Navi.
fleetd navi list [--archived] The Navis, oldest first; --archived includes archived ones.
fleetd navi rename <machine>/<navi> <name> Rename it. Its folder keeps its first name.
fleetd navi archive <machine>/<navi> Stop its lead and set it aside. Its chat, memory and folder stay.
fleetd navi unarchive <machine>/<navi> Bring it back. Its lead starts with your next message.

<navi> is its id or its name. A message to an archived Navi waits until you bring it back.

What it does on its own, and what it asks first

Section titled “What it does on its own, and what it asks first”

A Navi searches the web freely, and reads what its integrations hold and the factory’s status. It also writes drafts and notes in its folder, remembers and forgets, keeps its checklist, works screens and logs in with 1Password.

  • Files: it reads and writes what you can on the machine, except your keys and logins (~/.ssh, ~/.config and the rest), fleetd’s own directory and other Navis’. What runs outside its sandbox later stays read-only, so it can’t plant code that reads your keys: your shell’s startup files, your programs (~/.local/bin, ~/.cargo/bin, Homebrew’s and the rest), git’s config and hooks and the .claude, .fleetd, .mcp.json and CLAUDE.md of every project’s checkout, and its own settings in its folder. If a checkout is moved aside and replaced, no lead or agent starts there until you check it and run fleetd node trust <folder> (Security). A policy file’s allow rules don’t apply to a Navi at all. Its folder is where its notes, drafts and downloads go.
  • Its own home: each Navi has a home of its own, kept between sessions, where npm’s global installs, pip install --user, cargo install and install scripts put what they install, on its PATH. What it starts in the background with nohup or setsid keeps running after its turn, until the machine or the node restarts. It has no root, apt or systemd services.
  • Sandbox: a Navi’s Claude Code and every command, MCP server and tool it starts run in an OS sandbox that holds those lines whatever a command tries (Security).
  • Commands: its shell commands run without asking (decision navi.shell-unasked, D208). The sandbox is the guard. The one exception is a browser command that opens a site, which goes through the site rule below.
  • Sites: a link you write in your own chat message is allowed as you send it, so the Navi fetches or opens it without asking. A link it finds itself, or reads in an email or on a page, isn’t. The first time it fetches a page or opens a site in its browser, you’re asked in its chat, with the site named. Approve once and it fetches and opens that site unasked from then on. In a browser command the URL must stand alone, plain or in single quotes (agent-browser open 'https://example.org/a?b=c'). Double quotes, shell characters, an agent-browser open inside a longer command, or a flag that changes the browser (--session, --cdp) asks each time. www. is the same site, a subdomain is another, and localhost is a site per port (localhost:3000). Only http and https name a site: file:, data:, about: and other schemes ask every time. The MCP tools navi_sites and navi_site_revoke list a Navi’s sites, those from your links included, and take one back; a revoked site asks again.

When the sandbox can’t start, the Navi doesn’t run. Its chat says why and how to fix it. On Linux a Navi needs bubblewrap: install it (sudo apt install bubblewrap, sudo dnf install bubblewrap, sudo pacman -S bubblewrap). On Ubuntu 23.10 and later, AppArmor may also block the user namespaces bubblewrap uses; bwrap --ro-bind / / --unshare-all true says so, and Ubuntu’s apparmor package has a profile that allows them (/etc/apparmor.d/bwrap-userns-restrict). A setuid-root bwrap (ls -l $(which bwrap) shows rws) runs unprivileged for a Navi, so it needs unprivileged user namespaces too (on Debian before 11: sudo sysctl kernel.unprivileged_userns_clone=1). A Mac uses its built-in sandbox-exec.

Anything hard to undo, or that someone else sees, waits for you every time: sending an email or message, accepting an invite, making or changing a calendar event, uploading or sharing a file, spending, posting publicly, deleting, signing or accepting terms, changing a password. There is no “always allow”. Through an integration that’s fleetd’s rule. Through a command, a site or a screen it’s the Navi’s instruction: it runs such a command with gated_run, which asks with exactly that command.

This is enforced outside the model. A Navi’s lead runs in Claude Code’s manual permission mode with a short allow-list (Agent tools). Its file tools and fetches always raise a permission prompt, whatever your own Claude Code settings allow. The node answers the file tools and the shell commands. Every other prompt goes to the hub, which allows a site you allowed before or linked in your message. Anything else becomes an approval in the Navi’s chat, with the tool and exactly what it would get: the recipients, the full text. Approve runs the tool with exactly that input. Deny tells the Navi you said no, with your reason if you give one. A prompt that goes away on its own (the run ended) closes its approval. Moving money, trading and creating accounts through an integration are blocked even if approved.

What commands and screens can still do. The site gate covers fetching and opening a site; it doesn’t follow a page after that, or a command’s own network use. Browser commands inside an open page run unasked: clicks, fills, typing, snapshots, a script in the page. A command such as curl reaches the network unasked too: the sandbox limits what a command writes and reads, not where it sends. So commands and screens aren’t held to the approval rule for sends, and a click or a form that leads to another site isn’t asked. Its Android, iOS and desktop screens (agent-device) don’t ask per site at all. Nor is the final Send or Pay button on a site you allowed: that final click is left to you by instruction only. The Navi is told to hand it to you with a takeover, but that’s its prompt, not fleetd’s rules. Only allow sites where that’s fine, such as your bank’s when you mean to watch.

Sign-ins

Section titled “Sign-ins”

A Navi’s browser stays signed in. Sign in to a site once, yourself in a takeover or by the Navi, and its next browser session is still signed in there. No login page, captcha or approve-on-your-phone step each time.

  • What’s kept: the browser’s cookies and site data (local storage, IndexedDB), in a browser profile of the Navi’s own. Its history, cache, open tabs, saved passwords and form entries are not kept. Logins filled from 1Password aren’t saved either: each session that fills one still refuses to read it back.
  • Where: on the Navi’s machine, under fleetd’s node directory (browser/navis/<project id>). The Navi’s sandbox hides that directory, so the Navi’s commands can’t read the cookies. Only the browser fleetd starts for it uses them.
  • Whose: that Navi’s alone. Another Navi, a project’s agents and the browser in a preview each start with a fresh, empty profile that is thrown away when the session ends.
  • One at a time: one browser runs on the profile. If the Navi opens a second session while one is open in another of its chats, the second is refused until the first ends.
  • Its machine only: a Navi’s browser always runs on the Navi’s own machine, where its profile and folder are, never on another one the hub would pick.
  • Files: the browser touches no file on the machine outside the Navi’s folder. It opens no file:// page, whether asked for, linked to or redirected to. It uploads only files in the folder, not through a link out of it. It saves downloads only in the folder itself, each under a name of the browser’s own so no site overwrites a file there; agent-browser download <ref> ./<name> saves one under the name you give. A project’s agents keep the browser as before.

To sign it out of everything, open the Navi’s settings (Open settings in its right-click menu), find Sign-ins and click Forget sign-ins…. A browser session it has open now ends, and the profile is deleted. Its chat, memory, folder and allowed sites stay. Deleting the Navi deletes its sign-ins too. To sign out of one site only, ask the Navi to sign out there.

Telegram

Section titled “Telegram”

Talk to a Navi from Telegram, on your phone or anywhere else you use it. It’s the same chat as in the app: what you send there lands in the Navi’s chat, and its replies, photos, files and approvals come back to both places. The bot is yours and answers only you; nothing runs in a fleetd cloud, and your hub needs no public address.

Set it up

Section titled “Set it up”

Open the Navi’s settings (Open settings in its right-click menu) and find Telegram. It takes a few minutes, with no terminal:

  1. Make a bot with @BotFather. Click Open @BotFather, then send it /newbot. When it asks, send the bot’s name (the Navi’s, say Ada) and a username ending in bot (the page suggests one, like ada_fleetd_bot; add a number if it’s taken). BotFather answers with the bot’s token, a line like 123456789:AAH….
  2. Paste the token and click Save and test. The app seals it to the Navi’s machine before it leaves your computer: the hub keeps only its fingerprint, and it never appears in a chat, a log or the page again.
  3. Test asks Telegram which bot the token is, and shows its name, username and photo.
  4. Pair your Telegram. Click Pair with Telegram. Your bot opens in Telegram with a one-time link; press Start. Your account is now the only one the Navi answers, and its chat in the app says so. The link works for 15 minutes.

Once paired, the setting shows the bot, the account it answers and its status: Connected, Paused, Retrying (with what went wrong and when it tries again) or Stopped (a revoked token: paste a new one). Pause stops reading and sending until you resume, and what the Navi says meanwhile stays in the app. Unpair disconnects your account; Pair with Telegram connects one again. Replace token takes a new bot’s token, and Remove makes the machine forget it. To delete the bot itself, send /deletebot to @BotFather.

What goes through

Section titled “What goes through”
  • Your messages land in the Navi’s chat as yours, marked From Telegram. While the Navi works on one, Telegram shows typing….
  • Photos and documents arrive as attachments of the message: images, PDFs and text files, up to Telegram’s 20 MB for bots. Anything else gets a short reply saying it wasn’t passed on.
  • Voice notes aren’t supported yet: you get a reply saying so.
  • A forwarded message is marked Forwarded from …. The Navi reads it as someone else’s words, untrusted content, never as your request or approval, and asks you before acting on anything it asks for.
  • The Navi’s replies go to the app and to Telegram, formatted for Telegram and split when long. Its images go as photos, its files as documents. A table shows as monospace; a chart or diff says to open the app. A question with choices comes with buttons, and pressing one is your reply.
  • Approvals come as a card with exactly what approving runs (the command, or the tool and its full input; in a file just before the card when it’s too long for one message) and Approve and Deny buttons. Answer in either place: the other updates, and the card says who answered and how. A secret is never entered in Telegram: the Navi points you to the app.
  • Anyone else who finds the bot gets nothing back, not even an error. Group chats are ignored.

How it works

Section titled “How it works”

The Navi’s machine long-polls Telegram’s Bot API (getUpdates): no webhook, no open port. The token stays on that machine, readable only by fleetd’s node there, never the Navi’s own commands. The node keeps where it read up to, so a restart neither loses nor repeats a message; when Telegram is down or asks it to slow down, it backs off and tries again. Messages that come while the machine is off wait on Telegram’s side for up to a day. Replies the Navi wrote more than an hour before the machine could send them stay in the app.

Hub links

Section titled “Hub links”

A Navi can open and follow work in projects on another hub, such as a team hub when the Navi lives on your personal one. You make a link key on the hub it reaches and add it to the Navi. It acts as you there, and either side can revoke it. Security has the rules.

On the hub it reaches. Open Settings › Hubs, Hub links, Links to this hub (with several hubs, Links to that hub), and click Make a link key:

  1. Name the other hub as you think of it, such as home. Requests through the link read “you via home Navi” there.
  2. Leave Every project you can reach, or pick Only some projects and tick them.
  3. Make link key shows the key (flk_…) once. Copy takes it with the address other hubs reach this hub at: its HTTPS or tailnet URL. When the hub has no such address set, type it in the box that appears.

The list below shows the links made as you: what each is for, its projects, when it was last used, and Revoke. An admin sees everyone’s on Settings › Hub › People, under Hub links, each with the person it acts as.

On the Navi’s hub. Open Settings › Hubs, Hub links, Linked hubs, and click Link a hub. Paste what you copied into Link key: the address fills itself. Name the hub as the Navi should call it (acme), then click Link hub. The app seals the key to this hub, which checks it with the other hub and never shows it again. Then tell the Navi what you want (“ask the Shop lead on acme for saved carts”). It follows the order and tells you when it lands.

Each linked hub shows its address, the person it acts as there and its projects (only Shop, Website, or every project of theirs), and one word for its state:

State Means
Working Its last call went through.
Can’t reach This hub can’t reach the other one. The row gives the address and the reason, such as connection refused. A link needs the other hub reachable from this one over a tailnet or HTTPS.
Revoked The other hub refused the key: it was revoked there, or you left that hub. Remove it, or make a new key there and add it.

Remove deletes the key at once and asks the other hub to revoke it too. When that hub doesn’t answer, the notice says so: its link stays listed there until someone revokes it, but nothing can use it.

Memory

Section titled “Memory”

A Navi keeps your private life (family, health, money, accounts) in its own memory, never in your user memory. The agents of every piece of work you start read your user memory, so it holds only how you like to work. The nightly refresh tidies a Navi’s memory as it does any project’s.

Earlier in the chat

Section titled “Earlier in the chat”

A Navi’s new session sees only the latest part of its chat, but the hub keeps all of it. When you mention something from before (“the dentist I told you about”, “what we settled last week”), the Navi searches the whole chat by words, within dates when you give them, and reads the messages around what it finds. It reads only its own chat, never another project’s or another person’s Navi’s. The tools are chat_search and chat_read, in Agent tools.

Skills

Section titled “Skills”

A Navi learns. When it has to work out a task it will do again (where a portal hides the invoices, the steps of a grocery order), or you correct how it does something, it saves a skill: a SKILL.md in the same format as an installed skill, with what the skill is for and the steps. Nothing asks you first. When it uses a skill and finds a step wrong or missing, it saves it again, improved. The chat gets one quiet line each time (“Saved a new skill, weekly-invoices: …”), the name a link to the skill.

A Navi’s skills are its own: they reach its sessions only, never another Navi’s or a project’s. A saved skill is in its next session’s list as fleetd:<name>; the running session can read it from its skills folder at once.

Open them from the Navi’s panel, Skills, or from its Settings › Projects page:

  • The list: what it learned, what was installed for it, the hub-wide skills every project gets, and any in its folder. Each row says who changed it last, when, and when the Navi last used it.
  • A skill: its SKILL.md, rendered. Edit opens the whole file with a line for what you changed; saving makes a version of yours, which the Navi’s next session gets.
  • History: every version, newest first, with who wrote it (the Navi or you), when, and why. Open one to see what it changed against the one before. Restore puts an older version back as a new version, so nothing is lost. The hub keeps the newest 100 versions of each skill.
  • On: turn a skill off and the Navi’s sessions stop getting it, without deleting it. A Navi that saves an off skill again is told it stays off until you turn it back on.
  • Delete: removes the skill and its history.

An installed skill stays yours: a Navi doesn’t change it, and saves its own version under another name. The tool is skill_save, in Agent tools.

For the app and other clients

Section titled “For the app and other clients”

Navis are MCP tools on the hub: navi_create {name, machineId?}, navi_list {archived?, machineId?}, navi_rename {machineId, projectId, name}, navi_archive {machineId, projectId, archived?}, navi_sites {machineId, projectId} and navi_site_revoke {machineId, projectId, site}. A Navi’s or project’s icon is project_icon_set {machineId, projectId, icon: {face, color}}, a built-in face and color that replaces any picture; a summary’s icon says which it wears (one made from its id until someone picks). Its picture is project_avatar_set {machineId, projectId, attachmentId?, crop?} (an image from attachment_put and the square to keep; no attachmentId removes it) and project_avatar_get {machineId, projectId}; a summary’s avatar names the one it has. Each returns the Navi as a project summary with kind: "assistant", createdBy (the id and name of the key that made it) and, once archived, archivedAt. Every other project has no kind. Talk to a Navi with chat_send, as to any project. navi_asks {navi} lists what a Navi asked other projects for, with the orders opened for each, on a hub whose ready frame names the navi_asks feature. navi_signins_forget {machineId, projectId} forgets a Navi’s sign-ins; it needs the Navi’s machine online and on a fleetd that keeps them. Hub links are hub_links_get {navi?}, hub_link_mint {label, projects?}, hub_link_revoke {linkId}, hub_link_add {navi, label, url, sealed} (the key sealed to boxPubKey from hub_links_get) and hub_link_remove {linkId}. A Navi’s Telegram is telegram_get {navi}, telegram_set {navi, sealed} (the bot token sealed to the Navi’s machine’s boxPubKey), telegram_test {navi}, telegram_pair {navi} (the one-time t.me link), telegram_unpair {navi}, telegram_pause {navi, paused} and telegram_remove {navi}, each its owner’s only.

A Navi’s skills are its project’s skills (scope: "project", its projectRef), on a hub whose ready frame names the navi_skills feature. skills_list {projectRef} gives each with changed (its newest version: version, author agent or person, by, at, why), off and lastUsedAt. skill_get {origin, projectRef, name, version?} adds versions, newest first, without their texts; with version, its text is that version’s. skill_set {scope, projectRef, name, text, why?} is the person’s edit, skill_revert {scope, projectRef, name, version} puts a version back as a new one, skill_off {projectRef, name, off} turns one off in that project, and skill_delete removes it with its history. A chat message with skillSaved {name, version, created, why} is the line a save posts.
