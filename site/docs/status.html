    Using fleetd

    Status

    Status answers "how far along is this, and when will it be done?" for one order, one project or the whole fleet: a progress number fleetd verified itself, an ETA from measured speed, what the work waits on, what changed since you last looked, the fleet's health and the risks worth knowing. Every part says how fresh it is, and a part that isn't live says so.

    Open it in the desktop app with ⌘. (Ctrl+. on Linux), or from a terminal on the hub machine:

    $ fleetd status storefront
storefront  61%  polish 2/5    as of 14:02:07 (live)
  11 of 18 required items verified
  ↓ from 69% · +3 items: checkout redesign added (14:00)
  Switching payments to the new provider, waiting on the full test run. Nothing needs you.   [generated]
  eta Thu 14:00   planned Wed 18:00 (+6h scope, +14h slower)   4.1 pts/busy h, 18 samples
  waiting on  P12 full test run (serial); CI on #212
  since you last looked here 09:12  7 merged, 3 verified, 1 failed, +3 items
health  main CI acme/storefront green as of 14:01:50 · 2 of 2 machines online · claude 58% 5h 41% wk · 8 running, slots 8/40
risks   check `e2e (safari)` failed on 2 PRs today
doing   P12 full test run   T88   eta 14:40
        P15 provider cutover   T90   review of #212   eta 15:10
next    P14 switch the default provider (after P12), P16 docs page
done    P9 webhook outbox   #208 merged by the train 13:12 · check 2 passed C7

    fleetd status W41 shows one order and fleetd status the fleet. --watch keeps it on screen and prints each new version as the hub sends it; there is no refresh interval, so nothing changes unless something happened. Elsewhere, add --hub <url> --key-file <path>. See the CLI reference for every option.

    What the number means#

    The number is the share of required work that fleetd has verified, by size. An item counts only when fleetd checked it: its PRs were merged by the merge train (a PR merged by hand doesn't count) and the order's done-when checks it lists passed in the latest claim. A lead saying it's done moves nothing. The number reaches 100% only when every required item is verified.

    Items are must or polish. Polish shows beside the number ("polish 2/5") and never holds it back. Sizes are s, m and l, worth 1, 3 and 8 points, so a large item moves the number more than a small one.

    An order without a plan counts as one must item sized by its tier, done when the order is. It enters the figure with a marker ("+1 item: W41 (no plan yet)") and leaves it once it gets a plan, is stopped, or a day after it finishes. The view lists it as a risk after half an hour ("W41 has no plan"), since its number can only jump from 0 to 100.

    Plans and markers#

    A project's plan is milestones of items. The project lead keeps it with fleetd plan and links work to items with --item P12 when it spawns a task or enqueues a PR; you can run the same commands in the project's folder. A project's number covers its open milestones; a finished milestone leaves it with a marker ("M1 native runner done (12 items)").

    Scope can change, but never silently. For the first 10 minutes of a milestone the lead is still planning and items come and go freely. After that, adding, dropping or resizing an item needs a reason (--why), and the view shows it next to the number:

    72%  ↓ from 80% · +3 items: time inspector added (14:00)

    Items the hub closes itself get a marker too: open items of an order that finished without linking them ("−2 items: closed with W41, not separately verified"), or of an order that was stopped.

    How the ETA is measured#

    The ETA comes from how fast fleetd actually verified work over the last 7 days, never from the lead's guess:

    • Speed is points verified per busy hour. Busy time is when orders were running, so a quiet weekend doesn't make the fleet look slow, and CI, reviews and waiting on you are all inside it.
    • Samples: an ETA needs 5 verified items. An order with fewer borrows its project's speed, shared among the project's running orders, then the fleet's. Below 5 anywhere, the view says "measuring: 2 of 5 verified" instead of guessing.
    • Serial work: when items must happen one after another (--after), the ETA is at least the length of that chain, and the view names the item that sets it.
    • Planned vs current: an order or milestone keeps its first ETA as "planned". When the current ETA is later, the difference is split into what scope added and what slower speed cost: "planned Wed 18:00 (+6h scope, +14h slower)".
    • When the lead's own estimate is far from the measured one, both show, and the measured one wins.

    "Waiting on" names up to three things the work is blocked by right now, serial ones first: an item others wait on, CI on a PR, the merge train, your answer to an ask, a free slot, or a machine that's offline.

    Since you last looked#

    "Since you last looked" counts what happened in this scope since then: merged PRs, verified items, started orders, failures, new asks and scope changes. The app moves your mark when you leave the view after reading it for a few seconds. fleetd status keeps its own mark on each machine ("since you last looked here"), moved each time it prints or when --watch ends; the two never move each other. Marks older than the hub's 30 days of events read "more than 30 days ago".

    Stale data#

    Status never shows old data as live. Each part depends on a source, and the hub flags a source stale when it goes quiet for longer than its normal rhythm:

    sourcenormallystale afterthe view says
    a machinea heartbeat every 15 s45 s of silence, until it's back"no heartbeat from devbox for 4m"; after it wakes, "devbox slept 10m46s"
    Claude usageeach Claude replyits reset time passes, or its machine is stale"5h reading from 13:10, before the 14:00 reset"
    GitHubevery train pass (15 s) and main CI (60 s)180 s without a good answer"GitHub not reached for 4m: …"
    main CIread every 60 sits period plus 180 s unread"main CI of acme/storefront unread since 14:05"
    a merge trainevery 15 s120 s"the acme/storefront train hasn't run for 3m"

    A stale part keeps its last value with the time it's from and a warning, and the generated headline starts with "Not live: …" naming what's missing. In the terminal, the first line says "NOT LIVE: 1 stale source" and a stale line gives each one's reason. Just after the hub restarts, every source reads "not checked since the hub started" until it reports in, which takes seconds.

    If the app or fleetd status --watch loses the hub, the view greys out with "Not live: reconnecting · data from 14:02:07" and fetches a fresh copy when it's back.

    GitHub sync#

    The hub keeps track of PRs and CI on its own: each merge train asks GitHub about its open PRs every 15 s and about the main branch's CI every minute. Requests for things that didn't change are free under GitHub's rate limit, so this stays far below it even with many open PRs.

    You can also let GitHub tell the hub about changes the moment they happen. It needs a public URL that GitHub can reach and that forwards to the hub; fleetd never creates one, so bring your own (a reverse proxy, or a tunnel you already use). Then, on the hub machine:

    fleetd hub github-webhook --url https://hooks.example.com/github

    That adds a webhook to every repo the train merges into. Turn it off with fleetd hub github-webhook --off. With webhooks on, the hub checks main CI every 5 minutes instead of every minute; if it notices a change that no webhook announced, it goes back to every minute for half an hour and lists it under risks. A webhook only makes the hub look sooner: a lost or forged delivery can't change what it records.

    The headline#

    The sentence under the number is written by Claude (haiku, through your claude login on the hub, no tools) from a short list of facts fleetd computed: the number, what's in progress, what it waits on, the ETA and the top risks. Every number in it must appear in those facts, or it's thrown away. It's rewritten only for views someone has open, at most once a minute per view and 300 times a day.

    When the facts change and the new sentence isn't written yet, or Claude can't be reached, the view shows a plain template instead and labels it template with the reason. A generated sentence is never shown for facts it wasn't written from. Change how it's written with fleetd prompts edit status.headline (see What agents are told).

    Notification#

    The app can post one desktop notification at 09:00 and 18:00 with the fleet's number, its headline and what happened since the previous one. Turn it on in Settings → Notifications; it's off by default and respects quiet hours. Clicking it opens Status.
