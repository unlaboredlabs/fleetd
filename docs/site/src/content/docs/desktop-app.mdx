You'll pair the desktop app with your [hub](/docs/glossary#hub) and use it to chat with each project's
[lead](/docs/glossary#lead).

The app is installed with fleetd on any machine with a display. To add it on Linux without a display, see
[Install](/docs/install#options). It updates itself ([updating](/docs/updating-and-uninstall#app)); `fleetd update` updates it too. Shortcuts below use ⌘; on Linux,
use Ctrl.

## Pair it with the hub {#pair}

The first time it opens, the app says what fleetd is and how to connect to your hub.

With no hub on this computer yet, click "Set up fleetd on this Mac": it opens `fleetd init` in Terminal (on Linux, your
terminal). When setup finishes, come back to the app and it pairs by itself. If no terminal opens, the app shows the
command to copy. On the hub machine, the app pairs by itself
when it opens. On another computer:

1. On the hub, print a pairing code:

   ```console capture=pair
   $ fleetd pair
   Pairing code: 38X0-57P8  (single use, valid until 2026-10-18T22:43:43.690Z)
   Grants:       admin on every machine
   Hub address:  http://127.0.0.1:4778
   Pairing link: fleetd://pair?hub=http%3A%2F%2F127.0.0.1%3A4778&code=38X0-57P8

   The fleetd app on this machine pairs by itself when it opens. On another device, open the
   app and enter the code and hub address above, or open the link there.
   ```

2. In the app, under "Or connect to a hub on another computer", enter the hub address and the code. Or open the
   `fleetd://pair` link.

The code works once, for 15 minutes. Capitals and the dash are optional: `abcd2345` works as `ABCD-2345`. To connect with a key instead, click "Use a key instead".

The app gets `admin` on every machine, so it changes the hub's settings, such as max agents. For a device that
should only watch or only drive agents, print its code with `fleetd pair --role read` or `--role operate`. If
Settings says "This key lacks the admin role", the app was paired with less: run `fleetd pair` on the hub machine and
pair again with the new code.

To add another hub, or rename or remove one, open Settings (⌘,) → Hubs. See [Several hubs](/docs/several-hubs).

many projects need you.

### The panel {#panel}

Drag the panel's left edge to make it wider or narrower; double-click the edge to put it back. From the keyboard, Tab
to the edge, then ← widens it and → narrows it (with Shift, in bigger steps), Home and End go to its narrowest and
widest, and Enter resets it. Reading a pull request's changes widens the panel so the diff has room; that wide width
is set the same way and kept apart from the other. The panel never grows so wide that the chat loses its room.

That row is all the navigation the panel has: no tab has tabs of its own, and a tab opens on its list. In a narrow
panel the tabs show only their icons and counts (point at one for what it is). Each place in a list is a link too:
`#/project/<machine>/<project>/checklist` opens Plan, as the palette's **Checklist** does, and `/agents`, `/prs`,
`/artifacts` and `/activity` open their tabs the same way. `/sessions` opens Agents at its screens, `/train` Shipping
at what merges now, `/asked` Plan at the asked work, and `/decisions` the decisions, from History.

Click a project to open its chat. ⌘. jumps to the next project that wants you. To add a project, click **+** at the top of the sidebar (one more per hub with
[several hubs](/docs/several-hubs)). New project opens over the chat you're in, which stays as it was: type a name to
start a new, empty project, or pick a repo from the list (grouped by machine, then GitHub) or paste a GitHub URL,
`owner/repo` or a folder's path. Up and Down pick, Enter adds, Esc closes. The line above the button says what it will
do. You can also drop a folder or a GitHub link anywhere on the window, a chat included. Other files dropped
on a chat attach to your message. See [Add a project](/docs/projects#add-a-project) and [Start a new project](/docs/projects#start-a-new-project).

### When the hub is out of reach {#offline}

Your projects and recent chats stay on screen, marked "Offline, as of 09:02". You can keep writing: each message shows
"queued" and sends by itself when the app reconnects, once, even if you quit in between. Edit or Dismiss takes a queued
message back. The banner says why the hub doesn't answer and when the app tries again; Retry now tries at once.
Approvals, secrets and Stop wait until the hub is back.

Once you're connected again, a chat says "Last known" until its newest messages load; if they can't, it says so, with
Retry. "Disconnect and forget key" in Settings › Hubs & devices (**Remove…** with several hubs) clears what the app kept for that hub, queued messages included.

How to work with the lead: [Chat with the lead](/docs/chat).

## Agents {#agents}

The panel's **Agents** tab (⌥⌘B shows the panel) lists the project's agents at work under the orders they work on,
those waiting on you first: each one's role, its task, what it's doing and for how long. Under them, **Screens** lists
the [screens](/docs/browser-sessions) they use: the icons beside it open the Screens page and a new screen. Click one to watch its work as it happens: what it was told,
the commands it ran, the files it edited and what it's writing. Watching doesn't interrupt it. To change what it does,
tell the lead in the chat. Its PR number opens [the PR](/docs/chat#prs), and **What it was told** shows the exact
first message and prompts it started with; **All agents** or Esc goes back to the list. The [DMs](#dms) it sent other
agents and got from them stand among its steps as cards.

Under the project's own agents, folded, **Also on this repo** lists live work on the same repo and base that isn't
theirs, read-only, and **Other projects** lists every other project's agents at work, each project under its name,
those with an agent waiting on you first. Click an agent of another project to open its work beside that project's
chat. Neither is there when it has nothing to list, and what you unfolded stays so while the app runs.

The project's [Max agents](/docs/projects#max-agents) is in its settings (the gear at the end of the panel's row, then
**Agents**), or **Max agents** in the command palette.

## Merge line {#train}

**Shipping** shows the project's [merge train](/docs/merge-train) among its pull requests. **Needs a look** comes
first: the ones the train holds or sent back (a `hold` label, a conflict with the base, failed checks). Then
**Merging now**, a card with the pull request the train has in hand, its stage (rebasing onto the base, running CI,
merging) and the checks it waits on; pull requests that go through together as a batch share the card. **In line**
lists those behind it in the order they'll merge, each saying whether it's waiting for review, in review or ready, and
**Open** the ones not on the train. Merged and closed ones fold under them. Point at a row for what holds it, its
number and its branch; click it to open the pull request, which says the fix a refusal asks for. The list follows the
train as it moves and never guesses how long something will take. The tab's count is the pull requests merging or in
line. It only reads: the train itself decides what merges.

## To-do {#checklist}

**Plan** shows the project's checklist as its lead keeps it: how much is done, then what's being done now,
what's still to do and, folded away, what's done. An item's pull request or agent opens in the panel. The lead
updates the list as the work moves, and the view follows along as it does. Its count is the items still open.

You can tidy the list yourself. Point at an item for its delete button, or right-click it and choose **Delete**;
**Clear**, beside **Done**, takes off every done item at once and shows only while something is done.
Neither asks first: the items go at once, and **Undo** in the toast puts them back for a few seconds after, held
while you point at it. Quitting before then still removes them. The lead's next wake tells it what you took off,
and it leaves those off unless you ask again. To add or change an item, tell the lead in the chat. A project's lead
starts a checklist when it plans work; until then the tab says there is none yet. **Asked of other projects**, under
the list, is the work the lead asked your other projects for: see [Asked](/docs/projects#asked).

## What happened {#activity}

**History** shows what happened in the project while you were away, newest first: agents starting and
finishing, reviews, merges, what was asked of you and what you answered. A line marks what came in since you last
looked, and a run of the same thing (a pull request reviewed again and again) folds into one row. A line that names
an agent or a pull request opens it. Its count is what came in since you last looked. Two agents'
[DMs](#dms) to each other fold into one row per hour; a wake the guard refused has a line of its own. The filter at
the top right narrows the list to one kind of event; its **DMs** shows their log.

## DMs between agents {#dms}

A project's agents message each other directly ("DMs"): a writer tells the others what it found, asks the agent that
owns a file, or answers a question. A DM reaches a working agent at its next step, and its lead gets a quiet copy.
It never starts an agent that has finished, except to answer a question that agent is waiting on; otherwise it is
held for that agent's next run. Nothing about DMs shows in the chat itself.

| Where | What you see |
|---|---|
| An agent's work | Each DM it sent or got, as a card among its steps: "From T2573" or "To T2577" with that agent's title, the words cut at three lines (click for the rest), and what became of it: "Read at its next step, 23:02", "Held: it has finished…", "Woke it, 23:09: it answers what T2577 asked at 23:05", or "Didn't wake T2567: it has finished and isn't waiting on T2584. Held for its next run." The other agent's id opens its work. |
| History, filtered to **DMs** | Every DM between the project's agents, newest first: who wrote to whom, the opening words, what became of it (delivered, held, woke, didn't wake) and when. Click a row for the full words and a line per recipient, ending with the lead's copy. **Any agent** narrows it to one agent's DMs. |
| The agents strip | "8 DMs between agents" at its end, which opens the DMs log. |

Everyone in the project sees the same DMs, members and admins alike, and a device paired read-only sees them too. Nobody
can write, edit or delete one: agents write them, and they stay as the log. To change what an agent does after
reading its DMs, steer it or tell the lead.

## Decisions {#decisions}

**Decisions**, the line at the top of History, lists what the lead and you have decided in the project, one row per question with the answer in
force first. What it replaced, or that you revoked it, folds under it. Search, or narrow the list with the filter beside it to who decided and
where it applies; open a row for its why and history, and change or revoke the decision there. Its count is the
decisions in force.

## Settings {#settings}

Open Settings with ⌘, or the sidebar's Settings row. It takes the sidebar's place; Back returns to where you were. Type
in "Search settings" to find an option or a prompt by name, and press Enter to open the first match. Its sidebar is one
flat list of pages in three groups: **You** and **Projects** for everyone, and **Hub** for the hub's owners and admins
only. The rule: what changes a teammate's work outside your project is in Hub.

A row shows its title and its control. What an option does is its hint: rest the pointer on the row's title or control,
or on a section's label, or move there with Tab, and it shows under the title. A line stays under a title only when it
is state you need now: a current value, an error, a warning before something that can't be undone, or why a control is
off and how to turn it on.

| Page | Has |
|---|---|
| You › General | **Name**: what your teammates and the leads call you, changed in place: Enter or clicking away saves it, Escape puts the saved name back. Appearance (light, dark, match system) and time in the app. **Notifications**: which alerts show (the lead asks you something, a command or secret waits, the lead finishes what you asked, a machine problem, anything else), and a test. Their sound and the Dock badge, which counts the projects that need you, are your system's own switches for fleetd. There is never an alert for the chat in front of you, and in quiet hours only urgent ones show. **Version**: the one status Hub › Machines › Updates shows (up to date, an update to take, one going on), with its button; a member takes only this app's own update, and reads that an owner or admin installs the hub's. |
| You › Your accounts | **Claude**: which Claude account the orders you start run on, your own subscription (paste the token `claude setup-token` prints, then Connect and test) or the hub's API key when the hub's admins allow it (see [Claude accounts](/docs/people#claude)). **GitHub**: the GitHub account your PRs open as. **1Password**: your own service account token, optional, to [import from into the vault](/docs/vault#import), sealed to every machine, with Test, which lists the vaults it reads: it should read one (see [What an agent can reach](/docs/security#agent-reach)). |
| You › Vault | Your own logins, cards, addresses and variables, and the hub's, which your agents fill in and get without seeing them: a search, a filter by kind, **+** to add one, paste a `.env` or import from 1Password, and the open item beside the list, with Show (behind Touch ID on a Mac that has it) and Copy, its sites, who uses it, its activity, Edit and Delete. Only on a hub with the vault. See [Vault](/docs/vault). |
| You › Your memory | How you work, as the leads remember it: read by the agents of work you start, and edited only by you. See [Memory](/docs/what-agents-are-told#memory). |
| You › Hubs & devices | Each hub this app is paired with, with its address, its connection, its name and colour, Pair again and Remove; Add a hub pairs one more (see [Several hubs](/docs/several-hubs)). **Your devices**: the devices signed in as you on each hub, each other one with **Sign out** (after a confirm, its key stops working at once, with the hub links made on it, which the confirm counts; this app's own has none), and **Pair a phone**: a one-time code (15 minutes) shown as a QR code for the [phone app](/docs/mobile#pair), with the hub's address and the code under it. Then **Hub links**, both halves in one place: the link keys made as you on each hub, and the other hubs your projects reach (see [Hub links](/docs/projects#hub-links)). |
| Projects | Every project you can see, the archived ones last; click one to open its pages, as tabs, and **‹ Projects** at the top goes back to the list. **Project**: its icon and name, its repos and their setup, its agents' environment variables (a line with **Open Vault** on a hub with the vault), linked hubs, approvals, its one list of [MCP servers](/docs/mcp-servers), sign-ins, skills, payments, archive and deleting it (see [Project settings](/docs/projects#settings)). **Agents**: its [max agents](/docs/projects#max-agents) (the slider applies when you let go, with Undo, and "Use the default" goes back), the [task profiles](/docs/projects#models) its lead may use (the default is always checked) and its [skills](/docs/skills). **Instructions & memory**: its own instructions, added to its agents' system prompt after the hub-wide ones, then what its leads remember about the project, and the nightly memory refresh's last run with **Run now**. **Automations**: its [automations](/docs/automations). **Built-in prompts**, a row at the foot of Instructions & memory: the prompts fleetd itself sends its agents, the project's edits first and the rest by group, to open, read and edit for this project. A prompt with one text for the whole hub shows read only. See [What agents are told](/docs/what-agents-are-told). **Vault**: the project's logins, cards, addresses and variables, and its orders' variables, laid out as yours are ([Vault](/docs/vault)). |
